Our Literacy Strategy

BibleGap Replaces Sight-Word Guessing with Structured Literacy

BibleGap is inspired by the Science of Reading. Instead of sight-word guessing, kids learn systematic, explicit phonics so they can decode Scripture with confidence.

Walter Strickland·October 12, 2026

The national conversation about literacy has shifted dramatically. Investigative reporting like the Sold a Story podcast exposed how decades of sight-word guessing and visual cueing failed millions of young readers. When children are taught to look at the first letter of a word, scan an illustration, and guess what makes sense, reading fluency collapses, especially when they eventually face dense, non-illustrated books like the Bible.

Inspired by the Science of Reading

BibleGap is inspired by the proven principles of the Science of Reading. We set aside sight-word guessing in favor of systematic, explicit phonics and structured literacy. To read Scripture independently, a child can't rely on context or picture clues. They need the explicit decoding skills to sound out unfamiliar words accurately.

Why Scripture demands real decoding

Biblical text is full of unique names, ancient places, and rich theological vocabulary. A child trained to guess from pictures will quickly stumble on words like Covenant, Bethlehem, or Righteousness. Through structured literacy, BibleGap explicitly teaches sound-letter relationships and syllable patterns, so complex words are broken down into decodable parts.

From hesitation to confidence

When we teach children how English actually works through explicit phonics, we build true reading independence. Kids move from hesitation and anxiety toward fluency and deep comprehension. A child who learns to decode accurately gains a lifelong skill that unlocks academic success and a lifetime of reading God's Word with precision and confidence.

Your child learns to read by reading the Bible out loud.
